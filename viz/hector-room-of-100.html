Does more screen time mean worse academic outcomes in students?

★Very good ✓Good –Average ✕Below average How students say their teacher rates their schoolwork compared to classmates. Screen time is total TV, computer and games, averaged over a full week. Each student = 1% of real survey students.

About the data

HBSC international open-access files for 2005/06, 2009/10 and 2013/14, about 176,000–190,000 students per year who answered every screen-time question. The rating is self-reported and relative to classmates. It is not a grade or test score. Shares are unweighted and rounded so each room adds up to 100. Each room is a different group of students, so the page shows an association, not cause and effect. Screen time is each student's TV, computer and game hours added together and averaged over the week (5 weekdays, 2 weekend days). Correlations are rank (Spearman) correlations between that average and the rating. With this many students, even −0.05 is statistically significant (p < 0.001), but the size of the link is small.
