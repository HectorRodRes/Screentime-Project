Mental Health Across Time

Each dot is one real survey respondent, colored by generation. As the questions change, people move to the answer they gave, and each answer groups into a pie of generations. The left field is the 2018 survey and the right is 2024. Both years asked these questions with the same wording, so you can compare how each generation rates its mental health and stress.

Who answered what

Each field shows 800 adults drawn from that year's survey in proportion to the survey weights, so one dot stands for about 1 in 800 U.S. adults. The 2018 and 2024 dots are different people. The percentages on the fields and in the table come from the full weighted surveys (2018: 3,858 adults; 2024: 3,936).

Generations use fixed birth years: Gen Z 1997–2012, Millennials 1981–1996, Gen X 1965–1980, Boomers & older 1964 and earlier. In 2018 the Gen Z adults were 18–21; by 2024 they were 18–27.
